    How we tested it, and where it fails
    
      
    A finance tool is only worth trusting if it beats a simple rule on data it has never seen. Here is our test, including the results that went against us.
    
    
    
  
    

  
    
    
    
      
    The model
    
      
    For each of 31 assets, a ridge regression learns how its weekly return responds to the market, its own sector, interest rates and gold.
        Weeks when the market fell get extra weight, because assets behave differently in a crash.
        When a scenario fixes only some factors, the rest are filled in from how they moved together in past stress weeks (a conditional Gaussian expectation).
    
    
    
    
    
      
    The test
    
      
    Trained on 2010–2019 only. Tuned on two 2018 sell-offs. Tested on four later crises: COVID-19 (2020), the 2022 rate-hike bear market, the August 2024 carry-trade unwind and the April 2025 tariff shock.
    
      
    Baselines: “everything moves like the market” and the textbook CAPM beta.

    What we learned
    
      
      
        
    • Scenarios need detail. When the scenario says which sectors move, our model beats both baselines on average, though not in every crisis: during the COVID crash the simple moves like the market rule did better. With only the market falls X%, it is about as good as a textbook beta. That is why our preset scenarios describe several factors at once.
      • 
        
    • 2022 broke the old rules. From 2010 to 2019, bonds rose when stocks fell, so the model expected bonds to protect you. In 2022 both fell, and oil stocks rose about 60%. Any model trained on calm-era data carries that blind spot.
      • 
        
    • Company news is invisible. Meta's −62% in 2022 or Intel's −41% in 2024 came from company-specific events, which no factor model can see.
      • 
      
    
    
    
    
    
      
    Limits & responsible use
    
      
      
        
    • Only 31 US-listed assets. Bitcoin's behaviour is poorly captured by stock-market factors.
      • 
        
    • Scenarios are instant shocks with no time horizon, fees or currency effects (e.g. USD/KZT for investors in Kazakhstan).
      • 
        
    • The AI report can phrase things badly. The Veritas check catches made-up numbers, not every bad interpretation.
      • 
        
    • This project is a prototype and is not financial advice.
